SLIDE 1: PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE:
The Critical Blindspot in Disaster Telemetry & Public Safety Coordination

BULLET POINTS:
* Crisis Information Fragmentation: During floods, cyclones, and landslides, citizen
  reports flood unstructured social media (X, Reddit, local messaging) with missing
  coordinates and unverified descriptions, delaying emergency response.
* Vernacular Language Barrier: Disasters in multilingual nations like India create
  translation bottlenecks between vernacular ground accounts (Hindi, regional dialects)
  and municipal disaster command desks.
* Disconnected Evacuation Logistics: Displaced citizens frequently cannot locate
  operational relief shelters with available bed capacity, clean drinking water,
  medical stations, or wheelchair access during sudden emergencies.
* Spatial Silos: Meteorologist radar, atmospheric wind forecasts, and live citizen
  hazard pins remain separated in isolated portals rather than unified on a single
  actionable geospatial canvas.

SUGGESTED DIAGRAM / VISUAL:
Split contrast visual: On the left, chaotic unverified text posts with question marks
and missing coordinates; on the right, structured, geocoded GeoNetra incident pins
with severity tags and live shelter dispatch.

SPEAKER NOTES:
"Respected jury members and evaluators, during rapid-onset natural disasters such as
urban flash floods or coastal cyclones, every minute saved in situational assessment
translates directly to human lives preserved. Currently, disaster management agencies
face an information paradox: they are flooded with unorganized text across social media,
yet lack actionable, verified geospatial coordinates. Furthermore, affected citizens
panicking in rising floodwaters lack real-time visibility into which nearby evacuation
shelters actually have beds and clean water available. GeoNetra was engineered to solve
this exact operational breakdown."

VERIFIABLE CLAIMS & EVIDENCE:
- Aligned with National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) incident command standards.
- Grounded in empirical communication bottlenecks identified in the 2018 Kerala Floods
  and 2023 Cyclone Michaung post-disaster evaluation reports.

================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE:
Layered Full-Stack Architecture & High-Availability Persistence

BULLET POINTS:
* Frontend Client (Vanilla ES Modules & Bklit UI):
  - Zero heavy compile bloat; runs natively in modern browsers with instant auditability.
  - Design tokens: Obsidian (#09090b), Pure White (#ffffff), and Royal Indigo (#2C3480)
    complying with WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards.
* Dual Geospatial Engines:
  - Strategic 3D Planetary Globe: MapTiler SDK 3D WebGL projection with Open-Meteo Eulerian wind particles.
  - Tactical 2D GIS Canvas: Leaflet.js with marker clustering, heatmaps, and slide-in drawer.
* Backend API Services (Node.js & Express):
  - Stateless JWT authentication and bcrypt password hashing (10 rounds).
  - Structured generative AI schema validation using '@google/genai' v1.0.
* Resilient Hybrid Data Architecture:
  - Primary: MongoDB Atlas Cloud (Mongoose ODM with secondary indexes).
  - Secondary: Automated local JSON fallback ensuring continuous operation during network disconnections.

SUGGESTED DIAGRAM / VISUAL:
Full-stack architecture block diagram:
[Browser Clients] <-> [Express REST API :3000] <-> [MongoDB Atlas / Local Fallback]
       |                     |                            |
[MapTiler 3D & Leaflet]  [Gemini 2.5 Flash AI]   [Open-Meteo / RainViewer]

SPEAKER NOTES:
"Architecturally, GeoNetra is engineered for high availability and zero operational failure.
On the frontend, we utilize vanilla ES modules and semantic HTML5 with our Bklit UI design

JSON fallback subsystem: if an emergency response center suffers cloud internet degradation,

SLIDE 4: FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE:
Operational Feasibility, Edge Efficiency & Zero-Cost Scalability

BULLET POINTS:
* Client-Side Edge Compression:
  - Client-side HTML5 canvas compression reduces photo payloads by over 75% (< 400 KB)
    prior to network transmission, conserving precious cellular bandwidth in crisis zones.
* Mathematical Proximity Precision:
  - Implements the Haversine spherical trigonometric formula on Earth's R = 6,371 km
    ellipsoid, computing distances in sub-millisecond execution times without external API quotas.
* Cost-Effective Public Infrastructure:
  - Built on open, reliable data pipelines: Open-Meteo (open weather models), RainViewer
    (open Doppler composite), and OpenStreetMap Nominatim.
* Turnkey Portability:
  - Runs with standard Node.js and any lightweight static file server (Python 'http.server'
    or NGINX), allowing rapid deployment on field laptops or municipal data centers.

SUGGESTED DIAGRAM / VISUAL:
Bandwidth optimization chart: 4.2 MB raw camera capture vs. 320 KB compressed JPEG
payload over degraded 2G/3G networks, alongside the Haversine distance formula visualization.

SPEAKER NOTES:
"In disaster engineering, feasibility is measured by how an application performs under
constrained conditions. When cellular towers are damaged, uploading raw 5-megabyte camera
photos fails. GeoNetra compresses images entirely on the citizen's device before dispatch.
Furthermore, our proximity calculations to emergency shelters use local trigonometric
Haversine mathematics in the browser, eliminating reliance on paid, rate-limited external
routing APIs during mass evacuations."

VERIFIABLE CLAIMS & EVIDENCE:

SECTION 8: TECHNICAL VIVA QUESTIONS & DEFENSE MATRIX (FAQ)
================================================================================

Q1: "Why did you build GeoNetra with Vanilla ES Modules instead of React or Next.js?"
A1: "In emergency and disaster situations, frontend build complexity and heavy bundles
     are critical liabilities. Vanilla ES Modules load instantly on constrained 2G/3G
     field devices with zero build-step overhead, zero Webpack bloat, and 100% native
     browser auditability, while strictly complying with WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards."

Q2: "How does your system prevent false reports and trolling during crises?"
A2: "We employ a four-tier verification architecture:
     1. Client-Side Integrity: Mandatory GPS location verification and compressed photographic evidence.
     2. Community Consensus: Real-time upvoting/downvoting verification mechanism.
     3. Official Credentialing: Institutional observer verification system (/api/verified-reporters).
     4. Administrative Triage: Dedicated Operations Desk for report moderation and severity pinning."

Q3: "Why use the Haversine trigonometric formula over the Google Maps Directions API?"
A3: "Google Directions API incurs per-query monetary costs and requires stable external
     internet connectivity. The Haversine formula operates locally in client JavaScript in
     less than 1 millisecond per shelter, calculating great-circle distance on the WGS-84
     Earth sphere (R = 6,371 km) with zero API costs, zero rate limits, and 100% offline autonomy."

Q4: "Why perform image compression on the client before upload?"
A4: "Modern smartphone cameras produce 5 to 12 MB raw photographs. In disaster areas
     where cellular towers operate on battery power or 2G/3G speeds, uploading 5 MB fails.
     Our HTML5 Canvas pipeline scales and compresses images to under 400 KB directly in the
     browser, reducing cellular bandwidth usage by over 75% before transmission."

Q5: "How does the backend maintain data consistency between MongoDB Atlas and local disk?"
A5: "We implemented an active hybrid persistence layer in 'backend/db.js'. During normal
     operation, Mongoose writes to MongoDB Atlas with secondary indexes and 2dsphere spatial
     indexes. On cloud connectivity loss, the storage driver seamlessly reroutes read/write
     operations to atomic local JSON files, maintaining zero downtime for field operators."
